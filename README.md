# Fork-Friendly Freeware

## Please Fork, Modify, and Create Something New.

We believe software should be free to evolve.

Software does not have to remain exactly as its original
creator intended.

You are welcome to modify it, improve it, experiment with it,
or take it in an entirely different direction.

We don't just allow derivative works.

**We encourage them.**

## Our Philosophy

### 1. Software is a Starting Point

A piece of software does not have to be a finished product.

It can be a starting point for someone else's ideas.

Your version does not have to follow the original vision.

### 2. You Don't Need Our Permission to Have Ideas

You don't have to ask whether your idea is good enough.

You don't have to convince the original author.

You don't have to wait for an official update.

If the license permits it, just build your own version.

### 3. Different Directions Are Welcome

You might make something better.

You might make something simpler.

You might make something completely different.

You might even make something the original author
would never have imagined.

That's fine.

### 4. Forks Are Not Failures

A fork is not necessarily a rejection of the original project.

It can be a continuation, an experiment,
or the beginning of something new.

Multiple versions can coexist.

There does not have to be one official answer.

### 5. You Don't Have to Contribute Back

You are welcome to contribute improvements upstream.

But you don't have to.

Your project can have its own goals,
its own community, and its own future.

### 6. Commercial Development Is Welcome

We welcome commercial projects built from our work,
as long as they comply with the applicable license.

Free software should not mean
that everyone must work for free.

### 7. Credit the People Who Came Before

Software is built upon the work of others.

We encourage you to acknowledge the projects,
ideas, and people that made your work possible.

Respect the licenses of the software you use.

## What We Want

We don't want to control what happens next.

We want people to experiment.

We want people to learn.

We want people to create things we never imagined.

And we want software to keep evolving,
even when its original creators move on.

## A Simple Declaration

**Please fork our software.**

Modify it.

Improve it.

Break it.

Rebuild it.

Take it somewhere new.

You don't need to make the same choices we made.

**Make something of your own.**

---

## About This Declaration

This document expresses a philosophy,
not a software license.

The permissions and obligations for each project
are defined by its own license.

We encourage developers to choose licenses
that allow modification, redistribution,
and derivative works.

This declaration does not override
the terms of any existing license.
